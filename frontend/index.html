THE CORE PROBLEM

"Does financial news about one company secretly predict the stock price of another company days later?"

When major news breaks about Netflix, Amazon, or NVIDIA, market movements do not happen in isolation. News triggers a domino effect where systemic "Leader" equities set off cross-market reactions that ripple into "Follower" stocks 1 to 5 trading days later.

Objective: Discover which stocks lead and which follow using domain NLP and econometric causality.
Dataset: 73,936 financial news articles across 8 years (2013–2021) for 10 US tech equities.
Key Finding: Netflix news sentiment statistically predicts Meta price movements 1 trading day later (p = 0.0003).

THE 4-STAGE PIPELINE ARCHITECTURE (IN SIMPLE WORDS)
STAGE 01
notebook_01

Data Ingestion: 74K News & 8-Year Prices

Ingested 8 GB of real financial news headlines and 8 years of daily price history across 10 US mega-cap tech stocks (AAPL, MSFT, GOOGL, AMZN, META, TSLA, NVDA, INTC, AMD, NFLX).

Checkpoints: raw_news.parquet (73,936 rows) & raw_prices.parquet
STAGE 02
notebook_02

Domain NLP: FinBERT + LM Scoring

Scored all headlines using a specialized finance transformer (FinBERT 70%) and Loughran-McDonald dictionary (30%) running on Apple Metal (MPS) hardware acceleration.

Checkpoint: sentiment_daily.parquet (Daily sentiment per ticker)
STAGE 03
notebook_03

Econometrics: Granger Causality Tests

Computed pairwise cross-correlations (lags -10 to +10 days) and ran Granger Causality Vector Autoregression F-tests to prove statistical significance (p ≤ 0.05).

Checkpoints: lead_lag_matrix.parquet (10×10) & granger_results.parquet
STAGE 04
notebook_04

Graph Theory: Louvain Clustering & Signals

Used graph community detection to partition the universe into 5 Leaders (NFLX, AMZN, NVDA, AAPL, TSLA) and 5 Followers (META, MSFT, GOOGL, AMD, INTC) with Eigenvector Centrality influence scores.

Checkpoints: cluster_assignments.parquet & Interactive Graph
